There is a version of productivity advice that treats focus like an athletic event. Wake early, cut every distraction, track every minute, and push through. It works for some people. For most of us it lasts about a week.

I want to describe something quieter, which has worked for me for several years now.

## Start with the environment, not the willpower

Willpower is a poor foundation because it runs out at exactly the moment you need it. The environment, on the other hand, works all day without asking anything of you. I keep my phone in another room in the morning, and I write in a full-screen editor with no notifications. That's most of the system.

## Protect the first hour

The first hour of my working day goes to the most demanding task on my list. Not email, not messages, not "quick" admin. If the first hour goes well, the rest of the day tends to follow.

<PullQuote quote="Focus isn't something you summon. It's something you stop interrupting." />

## Rest is part of the work

Long stretches of concentration need long stretches of rest. A walk, a meal away from the desk, an afternoon that ends on time. These aren't rewards for working hard; they are the reason the hard work is possible tomorrow.

<Callout tone="warning" title="A caution">
If every day is a "deep work" day, none of them are. Plan for lighter days too.
</Callout>

## A simple weekly review

Once a week, I look back at what I actually did, not what I planned. The gap between the two is the most useful information I have about how I work, and it changes how I plan the next week.
