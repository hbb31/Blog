Last year I sdfgsdfgsdfgset myself a small rule: on weekends, I would cook one meal that took longer than it needed to. No meal kits, no shortcuts, no recipes that promised dinner in fifteen minutes.

## The value of waiting

Bread taught me the most. You can't rush a dough that needs to rise, and after a while I stopped wanting to. The waiting became part of the pleasure, a slow background rhythm to the weekend.

## Attention as an ingredient

When you cook slowly, you notice things: the smell of onions just before they turn, the sound of a pan that's hot enough. These details don't appear in recipes, but they are the difference between food that's fine and food that's good.

<PullQuote quote="The recipe tells you what to do. Attention tells you when." cite="PLACEHOLDER" />

## Bringing it back to the week

The weekday meals are still quick. But some of the patience has leaked into them, and into other parts of life too. I'm less likely to check my phone while something simmers, and more likely to just stand there and watch it.
