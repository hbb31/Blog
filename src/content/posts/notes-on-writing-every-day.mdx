_PLACEHOLDER ARTICLE. Replace with your own writing._

For two years, I wrote something every day. Most of it was bad. Some of it became essays on this blog. All of it taught me something about how habits really work.

## Counting words was a mistake

For the first six months I tracked my word count, and I hit my target almost every day. I also wrote a lot of filler to get there. When I stopped counting, I wrote less, but more of it was worth keeping.

## Small, fixed time beats big, flexible time

Thirty minutes at the same time each morning worked far better than "two hours sometime this weekend". The fixed slot removed the daily decision about whether to write.

<Callout tone="tip">
If you're starting a writing habit, pick the smallest amount of time you'd be embarrassed to skip.
</Callout>

## The archive is the reward

The best part of writing every day turned out to be the archive. Two years of notes, half-finished ideas and false starts became a well I still draw from.
